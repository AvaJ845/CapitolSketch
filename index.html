What it is

The trades Congress is required to disclose

Members must report every stock trade they, a spouse, or a dependent make — within 45 days, in a public filing.

CapitolSketch reads those filings, House and Senate, and lays every disclosed trade out in one chronological feed: who traded, what, when, the dollar bracket, and how many days passed before it was disclosed. Every row links straight to the original document.

It is not a trading app, a prediction engine, or a scoreboard. It restates the public record and stops there.

The feed

One feed, the same for everyone

No ranking, no personalization — the list is the whole public data set in one order. Nothing is selected or reordered because of what you happen to hold.

Each row carries a plain D / R tag and a House / Senate tag — never a colour. Switch to Just disclosed to sort by filing date, the part that is actually time-sensitive.

What stands out

A finding, not a leaderboard

The screen opens with the most notable thing in the current snapshot, stated as a fact you can check: how many trades were disclosed more than a year late, how many landed in the form's top brackets.

Below that: biggest trades, longest disclosure gaps, most widely traded stocks. Each list is a plain count. Nothing is scored against anything else.

The record

Every figure links to the filing

Amounts are ranges because ranges are all the form requires — never an invented exact figure, never a share count. The disclosure lag is on every row.

One tap opens the source: a US House Clerk PDF, or a US Senate eFD page. The detail screen names the filing it was transcribed from and tells you to check anything that matters against it.

Watchlist

It stays on your phone

Add the tickers you own and the members you want to follow. When a new filing matches, CapitolSketch tells you — with a notification that only restates the public record.

Your list is never uploaded. Not as a list, not as a hash, not as a count. No request the app makes changes based on it.

Members

The whole Congress, one plain list

164 filers across both chambers, each with party, chamber, seat, and the committees they sit on — drawn from public congressional data, shown as a fact about the person and never matched against a trade.

Filter the feed to one chamber, one direction, one dollar floor, or one state.

Craft

Calm by default

No account. No sign-in. No ads. No analytics. No brokerage connection. The app downloads one public snapshot — the same file for everyone — and everything else happens on the device.

Light, dark, and system. Three app icons. A version line that reads straight from the build.

How the data works

No backend
There is no server. A build-time program reads the primary sources and bakes one JSON snapshot that ships inside the app.
Same file for everyone
Two phones on the same version download the same file and make the same requests, in the same order. No request is shaped by the watchlist.
Kept current
On device, the app asks the House Clerk's own index which filings are new and reads only those PDFs — directly, over HTTPS, and nothing else.
Party & committees
From the open congress-legislators dataset, resolved once at build time and named in the App Review notes.
Senate paper filings
Hand-marked carbon-copy scans that machine reading can't do reliably. They are counted and disclosed as missing — never invented.
